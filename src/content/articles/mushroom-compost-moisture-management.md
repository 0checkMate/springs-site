Aim for compost at about **65 to 70% moisture** when you spawn, keep it evenly moist through the spawn run, and manage the casing layer carefully, because a button mushroom is mostly water and the casing supplies much of it. Both too much and too little moisture slow colonisation and reduce yield.

## Why moisture matters

Mushroom mycelium needs moisture to grow, but compost that is too wet loses the air spaces it needs and can turn anaerobic. Excessive compost moisture is listed among the causes of poor mycelial growth. Fruiting bodies contain about 90% water, and the casing layer is the main supplier of that water.

## Target ranges by stage

| Stage | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Phase I turning | Keep ingredients well wetted. One technical guide for the long method mentions around 75% during turning. Avoid adding too much water early. |
| End of Phase II, at spawning | About 65 to 70%. Technical guides give figures in this range. |
| Spawn run | Keep the compost moist. Do not water the compost directly. |
| Casing | The casing should hold plenty of water and be kept moist, but not waterlogged. |

## A simple squeeze test

Composters use a quick hand test to estimate moisture. Take a handful from the middle of the heap and squeeze it hard.

- Dry and crumbly with no moisture: too dry.
- One or two drops of water: roughly in the mid-60s to about 70%, the usual range for spawning.
- Many drops: wetter than that, around 70% or more.
- A stream of water or a pudding-like texture: far too wet.

This is a rough guide only. A moisture meter or an oven-drying test is more accurate.

## During the spawn run

Keep the compost evenly moist, and keep humidity high in the room. Technical guidance suggests humidity of around 85 to 90% during the spawn run. Do not spray water directly onto the compost, because this can cause rot.

## Casing moisture

The casing layer needs a high water-holding capacity. Watering should be gentle and frequent enough to keep it moist. A heavy stream of water can damage the casing surface and interfere with pin formation. Find out more in [button mushroom casing management](/grow/button-mushroom-casing-management/).

## Signs of too much or too little moisture

- **Too wet:** slimy compost, a sour or ammonia smell, patchy or slow spawn growth.
- **Too dry:** compost that does not clump when squeezed, slow colonisation, and casing that dries out and cracks.

## Common mistakes

- Adding a lot of water early in composting and losing control of moisture.
- Judging moisture by appearance alone instead of testing.
- Watering compost directly during spawn run.
- Letting the casing dry out between waterings.
