Oyster mushrooms grow on a wide range of agricultural waste, with wheat or rice straw the most common, and they do not need composting. They do need the substrate to be **pasteurised, drained to the right moisture, cooled and then spawned** with clean hands and clean equipment. Skipping any of these steps invites competing moulds.

## Choose and chop the straw

Use clean, dry, mould-free straw. Chop it into pieces of about 5 to 10 cm, which makes it easier to wet evenly, pasteurise and pack.

## Pasteurise the substrate

Pasteurisation reduces competing organisms so that the oyster mycelium can establish. Common methods are:

- **Hot water treatment:** soak the chopped straw in hot water at roughly 65 to 70 °C for about one hour. This is widely used for oyster mushrooms.
- **Steam pasteurisation:** hold the wetted straw at around 60 to 80 °C in a pasteurisation room for the time your method specifies.
- **Chemical or lime treatment:** soaking straw in a lime solution (for example, about 1% lime on the dry weight of straw) is described as an alternative. Follow safe handling practice.

Hot water or steam is generally the most reliable of these, though it takes more fuel and care. [CONFIRM: the method we recommend for small-scale growers.]

## Drain to the right moisture

After pasteurising, drain the excess water. The straw should be damp but not dripping, at about 60 to 65% moisture. A handful squeezed firmly should release only a drop or two of water. Compare with the squeeze test in [mushroom compost moisture management](/grow/mushroom-compost-moisture-management/).

## Cool before spawning

Let the substrate cool to room temperature on a clean surface before adding spawn. Hot substrate kills spawn. See [how to store mushroom spawn](/grow/how-to-store-mushroom-spawn/) for keeping your spawn in good condition until this point.

## Spawning rate and method

Guidance varies with the spawn type and the way the rate is expressed. Indian training material suggests about 2 to 3% spawn on a wet-weight basis, while other institute work uses about 10% of the dry weight of the substrate. These are close in practice, but the basis differs, so follow your supplier's instructions for the product you buy.

Fill bags or containers in layers: a layer of substrate, a sprinkle of spawn, and repeat. Press gently and close the bag to keep out contaminants.

## During spawn run

Keep the bags in a clean, warm room at around 25 °C. Technical advice from an Indian mushroom research directorate describes opening bags after roughly 15 to 20 days of spawn run, once the substrate is well colonised. [CONFIRM: our spawn run timeline for local conditions.]

## Common mistakes

- Using straw that is already mouldy.
- Not draining enough, leaving the substrate waterlogged.
- Spawning while the substrate is still warm.
- Poor hygiene during filling, which leads to green mould.
- Mixing spawn unevenly.
