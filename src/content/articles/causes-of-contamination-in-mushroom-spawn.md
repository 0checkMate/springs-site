Spawn contamination comes from four main places: the **grain** itself, the **equipment and room**, the **people** handling it, and **weak sterilisation**. It can never be eliminated by luck. It is controlled by clean process at every step, which is why laboratory discipline matters when you choose a supplier.

## Common contaminants

- **Green mould (*Trichoderma*):** starts as fluffy white growth, then turns emerald green. Healthy mushroom mycelium is tighter and rope-like.
- **Bacterial wet spot (sour rot):** grey, slimy, very wet patches with a sour smell, often at the bottom of grain containers.
- **Other moulds:** such as *Penicillium* and *Aspergillus*, which can appear in other colours.

## Where contamination comes from

### The grain
Research on button mushroom spawn found that grain itself can be a source of disease-causing fungi and bacteria, including *Trichoderma* and *Penicillium*. Dirty or damaged grain, or grain stored badly, brings contaminants into the process.

### Equipment, rooms and culture
Contaminated tools, containers, rooms or starting cultures can spread problems through whole batches. A contaminated pure culture cannot be used.

### People
One grower guide describes the human body as the biggest source of contamination in mushroom cultivation, through hands, clothing, breath and movement in the work area.

### Weak sterilisation
Grain that is not sterilised thoroughly will carry survivors into the spawn. An Indian study found that repeating autoclaving reduced fungal and bacterial contamination dramatically, with three autoclave cycles giving the largest reduction.

### Season and humidity
A study of oyster mushroom spawn recorded the highest contamination during the monsoon season, showing that warm, humid conditions raise the risk.

## How producers reduce it

- Selecting clean, whole grain and removing damaged grain before cooking.
- Thorough sterilisation, with the correct time and temperature.
- Working under aseptic conditions, for example with laminar-flow equipment.
- Maintaining pure cultures and discarding any that are contaminated.
- Inspecting spawn during incubation and before release.

## What growers can do

- Buy from a supplier who can explain their process. See [how to select good button mushroom spawn](/grow/how-to-select-good-button-mushroom-spawn/).
- Inspect every batch on arrival.
- Store it properly. See [how to store mushroom spawn](/grow/how-to-store-mushroom-spawn/).
- Keep packs sealed until use and handle them cleanly.
- Never mix a doubtful batch into compost.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming small coloured patches will not matter.
- Opening packs in a dirty or dusty area.
- Reusing opened spawn across several days.
- Ignoring contamination in the compost room, where it can spread.
