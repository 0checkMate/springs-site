Button mushrooms do not grow on raw straw. They need compost that has been prepared in two phases, outdoors and then under controlled heat, until it is free of ammonia, correctly moist and selective for mushroom mycelium. Compost that is not properly prepared is one of the most common reasons spawn fails to grow.

## What the compost is made from

Button mushroom compost is typically made from plant waste such as cereal straw or sugarcane bagasse, mixed with a nitrogen source such as poultry manure or urea, a mineral such as gypsum, and sometimes bran. Water is added throughout. The exact recipe depends on the materials you can get.

## Phase I: outdoor composting

In Phase I, the ingredients are wetted, mixed and built into a heap that is turned regularly. Heat-loving microbes break down the straw and the heap heats up. Guidance from growers in East Africa describes this phase as taking about 15 to 21 days, and the long method used in India runs for around 28 days with regular turnings. One technical guide gives a starting nitrogen level of about 1.5 to 1.75% for the heap.

Avoid adding too much water early on, so that you stay in control of moisture. See [mushroom compost moisture management](/grow/mushroom-compost-moisture-management/).

## Phase II: pasteurisation and conditioning

Phase II is done indoors, in a controlled room or chamber, and has two purposes.

1. **Pasteurisation:** the compost is held at around 57 to 60 °C for several hours to kill unwanted organisms. East African guidance describes holding about 60 °C for 8 to 10 hours.
2. **Conditioning:** the temperature is then lowered to about 45 to 50 °C for several days. Beneficial microbes use up the ammonia and convert it into microbial protein that the mushroom can use, making the compost selective for mushroom growth. Conditioning continues until the compost is free of ammonia.

Phase II commonly takes about a week in total. Good ventilation is essential, and more is needed in hot weather.

## How to know the compost is ready

- **No ammonia smell.** Ammonia kills spawn, so this is the most important check. If you can still smell it, continue conditioning or turn again.
- **Colour and texture:** dark brown, soft and granular, not slimy.
- **Moisture:** commonly about 65 to 70% at spawning.
- **pH:** roughly neutral to slightly alkaline, around 7 to 7.8.
- **Temperature:** cooled to a suitable range before spawning. Technical guidance gives figures from about 21 to 28 °C. Spawn is damaged by high temperatures, and temperatures around 35 °C are lethal.

## Common mistakes

- Spawning while ammonia is still present.
- Letting the heap become too wet or too dry.
- Cutting Phase II short because the schedule is tight.
- Poor ventilation during Phase II.
- Spawning into compost that has not cooled.
