When spawn is slow to colonise, the cause is usually one of five things: the **temperature is wrong**, **ammonia** is still in the compost, the compost is **too wet or too dry**, the **spawn is weak or old**, or **competitors or pests** are present. Check them in that order. Under good conditions, a button mushroom spawn run typically takes about two weeks.

## 1. Temperature

Button mushroom mycelium grows best at about 25 to 26 °C. Technical guidance recommends bed temperatures of roughly 21 to 25 °C during spawn run. Too low a temperature slows the run. Too high is worse: guidance warns that compost above about 30 °C is harmful, and spawn is killed at around 35 °C. Measure the temperature **inside** the compost, not just the air.

## 2. Ammonia

Ammonia left in the compost from Phase I can stop mycelium growing at all. If you smell ammonia when you spawn, the compost was not conditioned properly. See [button mushroom compost preparation](/grow/button-mushroom-compost-preparation/).

## 3. Moisture

Excess moisture is a recognised cause of poor mycelial growth, and compost that is too dry also slows colonisation. Check with the squeeze test in [mushroom compost moisture management](/grow/mushroom-compost-moisture-management/).

## 4. Spawn quality, age and rate

- **Weak, old or non-viable spawn** colonises slowly or patchily. A degenerating strain has the same effect. See [how to select good button mushroom spawn](/grow/how-to-select-good-button-mushroom-spawn/) and [how to store mushroom spawn](/grow/how-to-store-mushroom-spawn/).
- **A low spawning rate** may slow the run, though some growers report it does not reduce yield. See [how much spawn is needed per tonne of compost](/grow/how-much-spawn-per-tonne-of-compost/).
- **Uneven mixing** leaves patches without mycelium.

## 5. Competitors and pests

Areas where the compost is not colonised can also come from disease such as Pythium (sometimes called black compost), or from nematodes and fly larvae.

## Quick diagnosis

| What you see | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Whole bed slow, compost smells of ammonia | Incomplete conditioning |
| Whole bed slow, compost feels cold | Temperature too low |
| Patches that are slow or bare | Uneven mixing, wet spots, pests |
| Spawn grains with no growth | Weak or non-viable spawn |
| Compost warm to the touch, spawn thin | Temperature too high |

## What to do

1. Measure the compost temperature and correct it.
2. Check for ammonia and moisture.
3. Inspect for pests or competing growth.
4. If you suspect the spawn, photograph the batch and contact your supplier with the batch number.
