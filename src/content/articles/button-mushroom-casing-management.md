Casing is a thin layer of moist, non-nutritive material spread over fully colonised compost. It protects the mycelium, holds water and triggers the mycelium to form mushrooms. For button mushrooms it is essential, and managing its moisture and the room conditions after casing largely decides when and how well the crop pins.

## Why casing matters

Casing acts as a water reservoir, supports the formation of pins and gives physical support to the developing mushrooms. Because button mushrooms are about 90% water, and the casing supplies most of it, its water-holding ability directly affects yield.

## Choosing casing material

A good casing material is porous, holds a lot of water and has a pH in the neutral to slightly alkaline range, commonly 7 to 7.5. Peat mixed with lime is the standard in many countries, but peat is not available everywhere. Alternatives used by growers include:

- Garden loam and sand, around 4 to 1.
- Well-decomposed cow dung and loam, around 1 to 1.
- Aged spent compost, coconut coir (coco peat) and similar materials.

The structure should be crumbly. Casing packed tightly into smooth mud holds too little air.

## Treat the casing

Casing material should be pasteurised or otherwise treated before use, because untreated soil can carry pests and diseases. Indian extension guidance describes steaming casing at around 65 to 70 °C for several hours. [CONFIRM: the treatment method we recommend and its safety precautions.]

## Apply it at the right thickness

Apply casing once the spawn run is complete, and spread it evenly. Typical guidance is a layer of about 3 to 4 cm, pressed lightly. A study comparing casing depths found the best average mushroom weight and cap diameter at 4 cm. A layer that is too thick, above about 6 cm, is associated with poor mycelial growth in the casing.

## Temperature and humidity after casing

Extension guidance describes two stages:

1. **Case run (about a week):** keep the room warm, around 23 to 25 °C, with very high humidity, so the mycelium grows up into the casing.
2. **Pinning:** lower the temperature to about 15 to 18 °C and keep relative humidity at about 85 to 90%, with fresh air, to encourage pins to form.

Guidance varies a little by strain and system, so follow your strain's schedule. [CONFIRM: the schedule we recommend for our strains.]

## Watering

- Water lightly and regularly to keep the casing moist, not soaked.
- Use a fine spray. A strong stream damages the casing surface and can disturb pins.
- Do not let the casing dry out and crack.

## Pinning problems

Poor or uneven pinning is linked to low humidity, high carbon dioxide, high air temperature, a damaged casing surface and an unsuitable casing structure.

## Common mistakes

- Casing before the spawn run is complete.
- Applying an uneven or over-thick layer.
- Using untreated or poorly mixed casing material.
- Watering too hard or too late.
- Lowering the temperature too slowly or too soon.
