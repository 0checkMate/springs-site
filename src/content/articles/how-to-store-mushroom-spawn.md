Keep mushroom spawn sealed, cool and out of the sun from the moment it arrives, use it as soon as you reasonably can, let it warm to room temperature before mixing it into compost, and open each pack only when you are ready to use it. Spawn is living mycelium, and heat, moisture loss and contamination all reduce how well it grows once it reaches your compost.

## Why storage matters

Spawn is a living culture, not a dry input like fertiliser. Cold slows its metabolism and extends its useful life, while heat speeds up its ageing and gives contaminants a chance. Fresh spawn generally gives better results than spawn that has been held for a long time, so good storage protects the quality you paid for but does not replace using it promptly.

## What to do the day it arrives

1. **Inspect it.** Check the packaging is sealed and undamaged, and look for the warning signs described in our guide on [how to select good button mushroom spawn](/grow/how-to-select-good-button-mushroom-spawn/): coloured patches, slimy wet grain or a sour smell.
2. **Label it.** Write the date received, the species or strain and the batch number on the pack.
3. **Store it cool straight away.** Do not leave it in a vehicle, on a doorstep or in the sun.

## Where and how to store it

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| You have a refrigerator | Keep the sealed pack in the refrigerator, in the coolest part, at roughly 2 to 5 °C. Avoid repeated opening and temperature swings. |
| You have a cold room | Store the sealed pack there at a steady cool temperature, [CONFIRM: our recommended temperature range]. |
| You have no cooling | Keep it in the coolest, darkest, driest place available, away from sun and engines, and plan to use it as soon as possible. [CONFIRM: our advice for growers without refrigeration.] |

General guidance for growers is to keep spawn at about 0 to 4 °C if it cannot be used immediately. Keep the temperature steady, because repeated warming and cooling does more harm than a stable cool temperature.

Some oyster mushroom types are an exception: certain species, such as pink oyster, should not be refrigerated at all. Always follow the storage instructions for the particular species you buy. [CONFIRM: instructions for each of our oyster species.]

## How long does spawn keep?

There is no single figure, because it depends on the species, the grain, the strain and the temperature. Suppliers' advice ranges from a few weeks to around three months in a refrigerator, and the shorter end of that range is the safer planning figure for button mushroom spawn. The longer spawn waits, the more its performance can fall.

Plan your compost so that spawning happens soon after delivery. Order close to your spawning date, not weeks ahead.

## Transporting spawn

Heat is the main risk in transport. Technical guidance for growers warns that temperatures above about 32 °C harm mushroom spawn, and recommends moving it in refrigerated transport or during cooler hours, such as at night. Even research institutes that produce spawn ask customers to collect it in person, because long journeys by post or courier risk spoiling it.

- Collect or receive spawn during the cooler part of the day.
- Keep it out of the boot or cargo area of a hot vehicle.
- Keep it away from engines, sun-heated surfaces and other heat sources.

## Before you use it

- **Let it warm up.** If the spawn has been stored cold, let it come up to room temperature before mixing it into compost, rather than putting it in cold.
- **Open only when ready.** Keep the pack sealed until you are about to spawn, which limits exposure to contaminants and moisture loss.
- **Use the pack in one session.** Opened spawn is not ideal to keep for another day. Plan quantities so you can use each pack fully.
- **Mix thoroughly.** Spread the spawn evenly through the compost, as even distribution helps colonisation.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving spawn in a hot vehicle or in direct sun.
- Storing it in a place with large temperature swings.
- Opening packs early, or opening more than you will use.
- Keeping spawn for weeks beyond the supplier's advice.
- Mixing cold spawn straight into warm compost.
- Using spawn that shows signs of contamination because it seems a pity to waste it. A doubtful batch can cost far more in lost compost than it saves.
