Harvest button mushrooms while the caps are still closed, pick them gently, and **cool them quickly**. Fresh mushrooms keep respiring after they are picked, bruise easily and brown fast, so how you handle them in the first hours decides how long they last and what price you get.

## When to harvest

Button mushrooms are harvested by maturity, not just size. A good mushroom has a well-rounded cap with the veil, the thin membrane under the cap, still intact. Extension guidance describes harvesting at the button stage, with caps of roughly 2.5 to 4 cm, and quality standards group buttons from about 1.9 to 3.2 cm (small) and 3.2 to 4.5 cm (medium) in cap diameter. Mushrooms with open veils are lower grade.

## How to pick

- Hold the mushroom and **twist gently**, rather than pulling, to protect the casing and neighbouring pins.
- Trim the dirty stem base. Trimming the stem close to the cap also helps delay cap opening and browning.
- Handle each mushroom by the cap sides, not the surface, and avoid dropping or stacking heavily.
- Wear clean gloves, use clean containers and keep the packing area clean.

## Why speed and temperature matter

Mushrooms are easily bruised, and bruising shows as brown patches. They also lose quality quickly in warm conditions. Post-harvest guidance recommends cooling soon after harvest.

| Condition | Approximate shelf life |
|---|---|
| About 0 to 1.5 °C | 5 to 7 days at 1.5 °C, according to a university post-harvest guide |
| About 4.5 °C | About 2 days at 4.5 °C |
| Ambient room temperature | Often only 1 to 4 days, with refrigeration extending this to roughly 4 to 6 days, according to one study |

These are research figures, and real shelf life depends on mushroom quality, handling and packaging. [CONFIRM: the shelf life and temperature we recommend for our own mushrooms.]

## Packaging

- Use clean, ventilated packaging that protects against crushing.
- Avoid water condensing inside sealed packs, which promotes spoilage.
- Keep mushrooms away from strong-smelling produce, because button mushrooms can pick up odours.
- Label packs with the date.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until veils open before picking.
- Pulling, which damages the casing and neighbouring mushrooms.
- Leaving harvested mushrooms in a warm room.
- Rough handling and overfilled containers.
- Packing wet mushrooms in sealed packs.
